01 / What this is

An internship, not a course. You are given work, not lectures.

Fresh graduates are caught in the usual trap: no job without experience, no experience without a job. This program hands you the experience directly — six months of real tickets in a real codebase, reviewed by an engineer who has shipped software for a decade, with the certificate and project letter to show for it at the end.

What "mentor-led" means here

  • A working engineer, not a trainer. Ten interns per batch is the cap because every pull request is read and commented on by the mentor personally.
  • Real software, not exercises. You work on live product code and internal tools under an NDA, the same way a new hire would.
  • A monthly written review. Where you stand, what to fix, in writing — the same record the project letter is drawn from at the end.
  • A shared GitHub organisation and chat workspace that stay open to you after the internship ends.

Eligibility

You should apply if

  • You are in your final year or already graduated in engineering, computer applications or a related field.
  • You can write a small program unaided in any language. Everything beyond that is taught here.
  • You can give about 22 hours a week for six months, including the weekday evening sessions.

What we do not require

  • A minimum CGPA. We ask for it; we do not filter on it.
  • A degree from a tier-one college, or a metro address.
  • Prior knowledge of any particular framework or cloud platform.
  • Payment of any kind to apply, to interview, or to hold your place while you decide.

Ten seats means most applicants will not get one. Everybody hears back either way, with a reason.

08 / Questions

Before you apply

Is a job guaranteed at the end?

No. Nobody honest can guarantee you a job, and a programme that does is selling you something else. What is committed to here is six months of real project work, a mentor who reviews everything you write, an experience certificate and project letter, and two weeks of interview preparation. Whether AMPM Enterprises has an opening when your batch finishes depends on the year; interns are considered first when there is one.

Why does an internship cost money?

Because you are not producing enough value in month one to be paid for it, and a senior engineer's time spent reviewing your work has a real cost. Ten seats is the cap for exactly that reason. The honest framing: you are buying six months of supervised experience and the record of it, not selling your labour.

When do I actually pay?

Only after you are offered a seat and accept it. The application, the screening call and the technical task cost nothing. The booking amount of ₹20,000 confirms your seat and counts as the first month; the remaining ₹1,00,000 is paid in five monthly instalments on the 5th.

What if I drop out halfway?

You stop paying the remaining instalments — you are never liable for the rest of the fee. Instalments already paid are not refunded, and no certificate is issued for a partial internship. Withdraw within the first 10 days and only the booking amount is kept.

Can I do this alongside a job or final-year classes?

Yes — that is why it runs in the evenings. Budget about 22 hours a week: ten live hours and roughly ten to twelve on your own tickets. Interns who treat the personal hours as optional are the ones who miss milestones.

Are the sessions recorded?

No. Every session is live, which is what allows people to ask the naive question and show broken code. The trade is that attendance matters — 85% is the bar for certification, and missed sessions are caught up with the mentor in office hours.

Is the certificate a real experience certificate?

It is issued by AMPM Enterprises, on letterhead, stating the role and the six-month period, with a verification ID and a contact for employers who ask. It records an internship — not employment — and it says so. The project letter beside it is what gives a recruiter something concrete to ask you about.

Whose code do I work on?

Live product and internal tooling work, under an NDA signed at the start. Your specific contributions are recorded so they can be described in the project letter, and you keep the right to talk about what you built in interviews.
